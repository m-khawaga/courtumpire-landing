Understand scoring

Tennis tiebreak calculator:
who serves next?

A score alone is not enough to determine the server. Enter the service-order context as well as the points.

Singles: first to six games, win by two; set tiebreak at 6–6 to seven points, win by two.

How to serve in a tennis tiebreak

The player due to serve the next game serves the first point. The opponent serves the next two points. From then on, each player serves two points in turn.

First seven points in singles when Player A serves first
PointServerServing half
1ADeuce (right)
2–3BAd, then deuce
4–5AAd, then deuce
6–7BAd, then deuce

Deuce is the right half and ad is the left half as you face the net from the baseline. Start on the deuce half and alternate every point. Under the standard rule, both sides swap physical ends after every six completed points. Between points 6 and 7, the server stays the same, walks to the opposite end and serves from the deuce half there. The calculator gives the serving half and the change-of-ends timing. To know your physical end, track where you started and each swap.

What changes in doubles?

Keep the four-player service order from the set. For example, with order A1 → B1 → A2 → B2 and A1 due first, A1 serves point 1, B1 serves 2–3, A2 serves 4–5, B2 serves 6–7 and A1 serves 8–9. When a match tiebreak replaces the deciding set, the team due to serve still starts, and partners may reset their serving and receiving order as at the start of a set. Enter the order that applies to that tiebreak.

Set tiebreak or deciding match tiebreak?

A standard 7-point set tiebreak settles a set at 6–6. The calculator also supports a seven-point tiebreak at 4–4 in its four-game set format. If the match continues, change ends after the set. The side that received the first tiebreak point serves the opening game of the next set. In doubles, that team chooses which partner serves first.

A 10-point match tiebreak can replace the deciding set. It ends the match. Both targets require a two-point lead. Check the event rules for other formats, including 10-point tiebreaks played within a final set or alternate end changes after the first point and every four thereafter. The calculator uses six-point end changes and leaves service-order error corrections to the applicable rules.

Try these singles scores

  • 3–3 in a 7-point tiebreak, A served first: swap ends if you haven’t already. B serves point 7 from the deuce half.
  • 7–5 in a 7-point tiebreak at 6–6, A served first: A wins the set 7–6 in games. B serves first if there is another set.
  • 9–9 in a 10-point match tiebreak, A served first: swap ends if you haven’t already. B serves point 19 from the deuce half. An 11–9 finish decides the match.

Rules checked against ITF Rules of Tennis (2026), Rules 5–7, 10, 14, 17 and Appendix VI. This website supports the formats described here; event-specific variations may differ.
