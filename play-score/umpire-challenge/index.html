You’re the Umpire

Make the call.
Know the reason.

Choose 5, 10, 20 or all 100 original on-court questions from the bundled bank. Answers cite the ITF Rules of Tennis, 2026 edition. This is practice, not official certification.

Enable JavaScript to play the challenge, or read every scenario below.

Study the scenarios

Scenarios state their assumptions, including any doubles, alternative scoring or competition procedures. A new run shuffles locally without duplicate questions; refresh starts a new run. Submitted answers lock, and Previous/Next become available only after answering. For example, a 7–6 points score in a standard tiebreak is still live because a two-point lead is needed.

1. Standard advantage game: the score is deuce. A wins the next point. What is the new score?

Answer: Advantage A. A has a one-point lead after deuce. A needs the following point too to win the game.

ITF 2026, 5a

2. Standard advantage game: A has advantage, then B wins a point. What happens?

Answer: Deuce. The players are level again. The score returns to deuce.

ITF 2026, 5a

3. Standard 7-point tiebreak at 0–0: A is the first server. Who serves, and from which half?

Answer: A, deuce half. The first tiebreak point belongs to A and starts from the right (deuce) half.

ITF 2026, 5b, 17

4. Standard tiebreak: A served first and three points have finished (A leads 2–1). Who serves the fourth point?

Answer: A, ad half. A serves point 1, B serves 2–3, and A serves 4–5. Point 4 starts on the ad half.

ITF 2026, 5b, 17

5. Standard tiebreak: A served first and six points have finished (3–3). What is next?

Answer: B serves from deuce; change ends if not already done. The seventh point is B’s second point in this service turn. Players change ends after six completed points; the next serve is from the deuce half.

ITF 2026, 5b, 10, 17

6. Standard 7-point tiebreak: A wins a point at 6–6. Is the tiebreak finished at 7–6?

Answer: No, the lead must be two. Seven is the minimum target, not an automatic finishing score. At 7–6 the lead is only one.

ITF 2026, 5b

7. A served the first point of a standard set tiebreak, which finishes 7–5. Who serves first in the next set?

Answer: B. The first tiebreak server receives in the next set’s first game. B therefore serves, regardless of the tiebreak winner.

ITF 2026, 5b

8. A standard tie-break set is 5–5 in games. A wins the next game. Is 6–5 a completed set?

Answer: No. A needs a two-game lead before 6–6. At 6–5 the set continues.

ITF 2026, 6b

9. A standard tie-break set is 6–5. A wins the next game. What is the set score?

Answer: 7–5, set complete. A now leads by two games and wins the set 7–5.

ITF 2026, 6b

10. Under the standard change-of-ends rule, the third game of a set has just finished. What should the players do?

Answer: Change ends. Players change ends after the first, third and each subsequent odd game. The ordinary service order continues.

ITF 2026, 10

11. The server has won one point and the receiver has won two in a standard game. Which score is called?

Answer: 15–30. Call the server’s score first. One point is 15 and two points are 30.

ITF 2026, 5a

12. A board shows love–40. What does love mean?

Answer: That player has no points in the game. Love represents zero points, regardless of how many points the opponent has.

ITF 2026, 5a

13. The server leads 40–30 in an advantage game and wins the next point. What happens?

Answer: The server wins the game. The server now has at least four points and a two-point lead. Advantage is only needed after deuce.

ITF 2026, 5a

14. A player wins both of the first two sets of a best-of-three match. Must Set 3 be played?

Answer: No, two set wins decide the match. Best of three means first to two set wins. A third set is unnecessary after a straight-set win.

ITF 2026, 7

15. An event announces an advantage set without a set tiebreak. At 6–6, what follows?

Answer: Ordinary games until a two-game lead. An advantage set continues until one side leads by two games. A tie-break set is a different method.

ITF 2026, 6a

16. A singles event expressly uses no-ad scoring. Who chooses the receiving half at deuce?

Answer: The receiver. In this alternative, the receiver chooses the right or left receiving half for the point that decides the game.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, No-Ad

17. Under the ITF mixed-doubles no-ad alternative, who receives the deciding point?

Answer: The player of the same gender as the server. The mixed-doubles provision specifies the receiver of the same gender as the server. Receiving partners do not exchange positions for this point.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, No-Ad

18. The announced short-set format is four games by two, with a standard tiebreak at 4–4. What happens at 4–4?

Answer: A tiebreak decides the set, recorded as 5–4. This specified short-set alternative uses a tiebreak at four all. Its winner receives the deciding game in the set score.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Short Sets; 5b

19. A best-of-three event announces a deciding 10-point match tiebreak. The sets are one all. What is next?

Answer: The match tiebreak instead of an ordinary third set. A deciding match tiebreak replaces the final set. Its points are distinct from ordinary set games.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Match Tie-Break (10 Points)

20. An announced 10-point, win-by-two match tiebreak reaches 10–10. Which next two-point sequence ends it?

Answer: The same player wins both points. At ten all, a two-point lead is still required. Splitting the next two points leaves it level.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Match Tie-Break (10 Points)

21. Immediately before an ordinary service motion, where should the server stand?

Answer: At rest behind the baseline, between the relevant centre-mark and sideline extensions. The server begins behind the baseline within the permitted lateral boundaries. Standing on the baseline is not allowed.

ITF 2026, 16

22. A server releases the ball, decides not to swing, and catches the toss. Is that alone a fault?

Answer: No. A server may abandon a toss without attempting to strike it and catch the ball, including with the racket, or let it bounce.

ITF 2026, 19, Case 1

23. A server swings intending to hit the tossed ball, but misses completely. What is the call?

Answer: A service fault. Missing while attempting to strike the serve is a fault. It differs from choosing not to swing.

ITF 2026, 19b

24. During the service motion, the server’s toe touches the baseline before racket contact. What is the call?

Answer: Foot fault. Neither foot may touch the baseline or court during the service motion. Contact by part of a foot still counts.

ITF 2026, 18b

25. A server starts legally and jumps at contact without any prohibited foot contact or position change. Is being airborne a fault?

Answer: No, both feet may leave the ground. A server may have one or both feet off the ground. The other service and foot-fault requirements still apply.

ITF 2026, 18, Case 2

26. Which format permits serving from behind the space between the singles and doubles sidelines?

Answer: Doubles only. This lateral space is available for a doubles service stance. It is outside the permitted singles stance.

ITF 2026, 18, Case 1

27. A server makes a running approach as part of the service motion but never touches the baseline. Is avoiding the line enough?

Answer: No, changing position by walking or running is also prohibited. The foot-fault restrictions also cover walking or running to change position during the motion. Slight foot movements are permitted.

ITF 2026, 18a

28. A correctly positioned server faults on a first serve from the ad half. From which half is the second serve taken?

Answer: The same ad half. The second attempt uses the same half, except when the first attempt was made from the wrong half.

ITF 2026, 20

29. A doubles serve strikes the server’s partner before reaching the receiving court. What is that serve?

Answer: A fault. A served ball touching the server, their partner, or something they wear or carry is a service fault.

ITF 2026, 19d

30. In ordinary tennis, a server deliberately lets the released ball bounce once, then hits it as a serve. Is this valid?

Answer: No, it must be struck before that bounce. An ordinary serve must be struck after release and before reaching the ground. This scenario excludes wheelchair-tennis exceptions.

ITF 2026, 16, 19a

31. A receiver waits behind the baseline and outside the singles sideline, on their own side of the net. Is this position prohibited?

Answer: No, the receiver may stand inside or outside the court on that side. The receiver is not confined to the marked court. The server’s positioning restrictions do not apply to the receiver.

ITF 2026, 8, Case 1

32. A receiver attempts a return, misses, then says they were not ready. What does the attempt establish?

Answer: The receiver is considered ready. An attempt to return establishes readiness. A later claim does not undo that attempt.

ITF 2026, 21

33. A serve is delivered to a demonstrably unready receiver who makes no return attempt. The ball lands long. What is the treatment?

Answer: A service let; it cannot be called a fault. The server must wait for readiness. When the receiver is shown not to have been ready, the serve is a let rather than a fault.

ITF 2026, 21, 22b

34. A receiver volleys a serve before it bounces and returns it into court. Who wins the point?

Answer: The server. A serve must bounce before it is returned. A successful volley does not meet that requirement.

ITF 2026, 24e

35. A doubles serve, without touching the net, hits the receiver’s partner before bouncing. The partner is outside the service box. Who wins?

Answer: The serving team. Without a service let, hitting either receiver before the first bounce gives the server the point. Standing outside the box does not change this.

ITF 2026, 24, Case 7

36. A1 serves game 1 and B1 serves game 2 of a doubles set. Who serves games 3 and 4?

Answer: A2 then B2. Partners alternate their team’s service games in the established sequence: A1, B1, A2, B2.

ITF 2026, 14

37. A1 has received the first point of a doubles set, establishing the receiving order. May A2 take a later deuce-court return merely to improve the matchup?

Answer: No, follow the receiving order chosen for the set. The designated receiving rotation continues through the set. It is not a fresh choice before each point.

ITF 2026, 15

38. A doubles return brushes one partner’s racket, then is hit by the other partner before crossing the net. What happens?

Answer: That team loses the point. Both partners cannot touch the ball as part of returning it. This differs from one player’s accidental double contact.

ITF 2026, 24l

39. A doubles server’s partner stands near the net on their own side, inside a service box, and causes no hindrance. Is this allowed?

Answer: Yes. The non-serving and non-receiving partners may stand anywhere on their own side, inside or outside the court, subject to hindrance rules.

ITF 2026, 26, Case 5

40. At the start of a new doubles set, the team due to serve wants its other partner to serve first for that team. Is this allowed?

Answer: Yes, each team chooses its first server for each set. The team may choose its first server at each set’s start. The teams’ overall service turns still apply.

ITF 2026, 14

41. Part of a rally ball touches the baseline on landing. Is it in or out?

Answer: In. Touching a boundary line counts as touching the court it bounds. There is no majority-of-the-ball test.

ITF 2026, 12

42. A serve lands on the centre service line bounding the correct diagonal box. What is the call?

Answer: Good serve. The line bounds the service box. Touching it is in if the serve is otherwise valid.

ITF 2026, 12, 17

43. A doubles serve first bounces in the alley, beyond the singles sideline but inside the doubles sideline. Is the extra width available for serving?

Answer: No, service boxes retain their usual width. Doubles alleys enlarge the rally court, not the service boxes. The serve must first bounce in the correct service court.

ITF 2026, 1, 17

44. In a singles rally, the ball lands in the doubles alley without touching the singles sideline. What is the decision?

Answer: Out. The singles court ends at its singles sideline. The alley is outside even on a court also marked for doubles.

ITF 2026, 1, 24c

45. A ball bounces in your court and spins back over the net. May you reach across to return it without touching the net or opponent’s court?

Answer: Yes, this bounce-back situation permits reaching over. The rules allow reaching across when a valid bounce sends the ball back over the net. Other contact restrictions remain.

ITF 2026, 25b

46. In doubles, a return goes outside a net post below net-top height and bounces correctly, touching no permanent fixture. Is it good?

Answer: Yes, this return need not pass above net height. A return may pass outside the posts above or below net height, provided it lands correctly and meets the other return rules.

ITF 2026, 25c

47. A player hits on their own side, then follows through above the net without touching it. The return lands in. What happens?

Answer: Play continues. The racket may pass above the net after contact on the player’s own side. A net touch would be a separate issue.

ITF 2026, 25e

48. One continuous, unintentional stroke accidentally contacts the same player’s racket twice and lands in. Is that alone a loss of point?

Answer: No, deliberately repeated contact is the prohibited case. The rule prohibits deliberate carrying or repeated contact. An accidental double hit is not itself a hindrance.

ITF 2026, 24f; 26, Case 1

49. A player catches a high rally ball before its bounce, saying it was obviously heading out. What is the decision?

Answer: The catching player loses the point. Predicting an out bounce does not end the rally. Catching a live ball gives away the point.

ITF 2026, 24, Case 8

50. A thrown racket strikes the ball into court for an apparent winner. Is that a valid return?

Answer: No, the player loses the point. The racket must be held when contacting the ball. Throwing it to return the ball loses the point.

ITF 2026, 24j, Case 6

51. A player’s shirt touches the net while the ball is in play, although the racket stays clear. What happens?

Answer: The player loses the point. The net-touch rule covers the player and anything worn or carried, not just the racket.

ITF 2026, 24g

52. A live rally ball clips a player’s shoe before a successful racket return. What is the decision?

Answer: The player struck loses the point. The live ball may not touch the player or something worn or carried, apart from the held racket. A later racket hit cannot repair it.

ITF 2026, 24i

53. An incoming ball has not crossed the net and has not bounced back from the volleyer’s side. The volleyer reaches over and hits it. Is this legal?

Answer: No, the volleyer loses the point. Ordinary contact must wait for the ball to pass the net. The special bounced-back-ball exception is excluded here.

ITF 2026, 24h, 25b

54. A return hits the opponent’s backstop before bouncing on the court. Who wins?

Answer: The opponent. A backstop is a permanent fixture. Hitting it before a valid bounce loses the point for the hitter.

ITF 2026, 2, 13

55. An untouched return bounces in the correct court and then hits the backstop. Who wins?

Answer: The player who made the return. After the unreturned ball has bounced correctly, contact with a permanent fixture awards the point to the hitter.

ITF 2026, 13

56. A return hits a spare ball lying in the opponents’ correct court. Everyone can still identify the actual ball in play. What happens?

Answer: Continue the point. This can be a good return. A let is needed if the actual ball becomes unclear, which this scenario excludes.

ITF 2026, 25f, Case 2

57. The ball splits open during a rally. What happens to that point?

Answer: Replay the point. A ball breaking during play requires a replay. This differs from a merely soft ball.

ITF 2026, 3

58. After a point, the ball feels soft but is not broken. Must that completed point be replayed for this reason?

Answer: No. A soft but unbroken ball does not require replaying the completed point. The broken-ball provision is narrower.

ITF 2026, 3, Case 1

59. A string breaks during a point. No event regulation forbids broken-string rackets. May the player continue using it?

Answer: Yes. Continuing with accidentally broken strings is permitted unless event organizers specifically prohibit it.

ITF 2026, 4, Case 4

60. Where may a string vibration damper be attached under the racket rule?

Answer: Outside the crossed-string pattern. The permitted string location is outside the pattern of crossed strings, not arbitrarily among them.

ITF 2026, 4, Case 3

61. Under ordinary service-let rules, a first serve touches the net and lands correctly. Which attempt follows?

Answer: Another first serve. An otherwise good net-touching serve is repeated as the same service attempt. No fault has been used.

ITF 2026, 22a

62. After a first-serve fault, the second serve clips the net and lands correctly. What follows under ordinary service-let rules?

Answer: Another second serve. A service let does not cancel the earlier first-serve fault. Only the second attempt is repeated.

ITF 2026, 22

63. A serve clips the net and first bounces outside the correct box. Under ordinary service rules, what is the call?

Answer: Fault. Net contact creates a service let only when the serve is otherwise good. This bounce is not.

ITF 2026, 22a, 17

64. A groundstroke clips the net cord and lands in the correct court. Is net contact alone a reason to replay?

Answer: No, continue. Rally returns may touch the net and still be good. The service-let rule does not apply to groundstrokes.

ITF 2026, 25a

65. The first serve was a fault. During the following rally, a ball rolls in from another court and a let is called. How is the point restarted?

Answer: With a first serve. A rally let restarts the whole point, unlike a second-service let that preserves the earlier fault.

ITF 2026, 23, Case 1

66. The first serve is a fault and the next service attempt is also a fault, with no let between them. What happens?

Answer: The receiver wins the point. Two consecutive service faults lose the point for the server. There is no third attempt.

ITF 2026, 24a

67. An event explicitly uses the Appendix VI no-let service alternative. A serve clips the net and lands correctly. What happens?

Answer: The ball remains in play. This announced alternative removes the ordinary net-touch service let for an otherwise valid serve. It is not the default rule.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, No Let

68. An umpire finds an opponent’s unintentional act genuinely hindered a shot. No other exception applies. What is the general result?

Answer: Replay the point. An unintentional opponent act causing hindrance generally produces a replay. A deliberate act has a different consequence.

ITF 2026, 26

69. An umpire finds a player deliberately distracted the opponent and actually hindered the opponent’s shot. Who wins?

Answer: The hindered opponent. An opponent’s deliberate act causing hindrance awards the point to the hindered player. Actual hindrance is part of the stated finding.

ITF 2026, 26

70. A live rally ball hits a bird flying across the court. What is the outcome?

Answer: Replay the point. This is outside interference requiring a replay. A permanent fixture such as a backstop is different.

ITF 2026, 26, Case 3

71. A spare ball was already lying on a player’s own side when the point began. It later obstructs that player. Is that alone a valid hindrance claim?

Answer: No. This case excludes an object already present on the player’s side at the start. Remove such objects before beginning a point.

ITF 2026, 26, Case 4

72. A player stops a rally solely because they think the opponent was being hindered. Does that belief justify stopping?

Answer: No, the player who stopped loses the point. The specific case does not allow stopping on the assumption that the opponent was hindered.

ITF 2026, 26, Case 2

73. A doubles receiver’s partner touches the net while the serve is travelling, before its first bounce. What is the decision?

Answer: The receiving team loses the point. The ball is live after service contact. The receiving partner’s net touch before the bounce loses the point.

ITF 2026, 24g, Case 3

74. In singles with a doubles net and singles sticks, a return passes under the cord between a stick and adjacent post, touches neither, and lands in. Is it good?

Answer: Yes. The rules specifically permit this route if the ball touches no net, cord or post and lands correctly.

ITF 2026, 25d

75. A player runs outside a net post across the imaginary sideways extension of the net, without touching the opponent’s court or forbidden objects. Is that crossing alone a loss?

Answer: No. Crossing the imaginary extension alone does not lose the point. Touching the opponent’s court or net during play would be different.

ITF 2026, 24, Case 4

76. May a player hold and use two rackets at once during play?

Answer: No. Using more than one racket at a time is prohibited. Changing rackets between points is a different matter.

ITF 2026, 4, Case 5

77. A proposed racket has a battery that alters its playing characteristics. Is that allowed?

Answer: No. An energy source affecting playing characteristics is prohibited. Opponent agreement does not change that requirement.

ITF 2026, 4, Case 6

78. When should the initial choice of court ends and service be decided by toss?

Answer: Before the warm-up. The toss precedes warm-up so the starting choices can be established.

ITF 2026, 9

79. The toss winner chooses to serve first. Who chooses the initial court end?

Answer: The opponent. Choosing service leaves the end choice to the opponent. Winning the toss does not automatically give both choices.

ITF 2026, 9a

80. An announced ball change falls at the beginning of a set tiebreak. When is it made?

Answer: At the start of the second game of the next set. A scheduled ball change is delayed rather than made at the beginning of a tiebreak.

ITF 2026, 3

81. A set ends 6–2, an even total of eight games. Under the standard rule, when is the next end change?

Answer: After the next set’s first game. With an even game total, keep the same ends at the set boundary and change after the next set’s first game.

ITF 2026, 10

82. In an umpired match, a player disputes the chair umpire’s interpretation of tennis law, rather than a factual line call. May the player ask for the referee?

Answer: Yes, the referee is the final authority on tennis-law questions. The rules distinguish questions of law from questions of fact. A player may request the referee over the chair’s interpretation of tennis law.

ITF 2026, Appendix VII

83. The first game of a set ends. Under ordinary continuous-play rules, what accompanies the end change?

Answer: No rest. Players change ends after the first game but continue without the usual changeover rest.

ITF 2026, 10, 29a

84. Six points of a standard set tiebreak are complete. May players take the ordinary 90-second rest while changing ends?

Answer: No. The standard six-point end change is made without a rest during the tiebreak.

ITF 2026, 10, 29a

85. A deciding match tiebreak replaces Set 3. May the singles players toss again to choose a different first server?

Answer: No, the existing service order continues. A match tiebreak continues the original service order. It does not start a new match with another toss.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Match Tie-Break notes

86. Under the Appendix VI deciding match-tiebreak procedure, what break comes immediately before it?

Answer: A 120-second set break. The match-tiebreak notes specify a 120-second set break. Any other special event provision must be considered separately.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Match Tie-Break notes

87. A short-set event expressly uses the Appendix VI five-point short-set tiebreak. At 4–4 in points, what happens?

Answer: Play one deciding point. This specific alternative has a deciding point at four all. It differs from the standard seven-point, win-by-two tiebreak.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Short Set Tie-Break (5 Points)

88. An event chooses four-game short sets. Does that choice alone make every game no-ad?

Answer: No, game scoring must also be specified. Set length and no-ad game scoring are separate alternatives. The event must specify the game-scoring method.

ITF 2026, 5a; Appendix VI, No-Ad and Short Sets

89. The announced format uses a 10-point final-set tiebreak at 6–6, rather than a match tiebreak replacing the set. At one set all, what is next?

Answer: A full final set, with that tiebreak only if games reach 6–6. A final-set tiebreak is played within a full final set. A deciding match tiebreak replaces that set entirely.

ITF 2026, Appendix VI, Final Set Tie-Break (10 Points)

90. A server faults from the wrong half. The mistake is found before the second serve. What happens?

Answer: Take the second serve from the correct half; the fault stands. Correct the serving half immediately. A fault served from the wrong half remains a fault.

ITF 2026, 27a

91. Both players are at the wrong court ends. A first-serve fault occurs before this is found. What is the correction?

Answer: Move to the correct ends and give a first serve. Correct the ends according to the score. Unlike a wrong-half error, the fault made before this error was found does not stand.

ITF 2026, 27b

92. In singles, the wrong player serves an entire ordinary game before the error is noticed. What happens to service order?

Answer: Keep the altered order. After an ordinary game is completed by the wrong server, the altered order remains. Completed points also stand.

ITF 2026, 27c

93. In singles, the wrong server is discovered after two completed points of an ordinary game. What is the basic correction?

Answer: The proper server takes over; completed points stand. Before a game is complete, restore the proper server when the error is found. Completed points are not erased.

ITF 2026, 27c

94. The wrong tiebreak server is discovered after an even number of points have been completed. Should the error be corrected now?

Answer: Yes, immediately. The correction depends on completed-point parity: correct after an even number; the altered order remains after an odd number.

ITF 2026, 27d

95. During a doubles game, partners discover they have reversed receiving order. What should they do?

Answer: Keep it for this game, then restore the proper order next receiving game. The receiving error remains for that game. Resume the original receiving order in their next receiving game.

ITF 2026, 27e

96. Under the ordinary continuous-play rule, what is the maximum time from a point ending until the next first service is struck?

Answer: 25 seconds. The baseline between-point limit is 25 seconds. Applicable event procedures describe its administration.

ITF 2026, 29a

97. No special extension applies. What is the maximum rest at an eligible ordinary-game changeover, excluding the first game of a set?

Answer: 90 seconds. An eligible normal changeover allows up to 90 seconds. The first game and tiebreak end changes are exceptions without rest.

ITF 2026, 29a

98. No special event extension applies. What is the ordinary maximum break at the end of a set?

Answer: 120 seconds. The usual set break is 120 seconds, measured until the next first service is struck.

ITF 2026, 29a

99. The organizers have not set a different warm-up provision. What maximum do the ordinary rules specify?

Answer: Five minutes. The baseline warm-up maximum is five minutes, unless organizers decide otherwise.

ITF 2026, 29e

100. A player assumes off-court coaching is automatically allowed at every 2026 event. What should be checked?

Answer: Whether the sanctioning body permits it and under what conditions. Off-court coaching may be permitted by the sanctioning body. The applicable event conditions therefore matter.
