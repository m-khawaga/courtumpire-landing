# Activity content and diagrams

The static catalogue contains **50 activities**: the original 16 retain their IDs and URLs, and 34 original additions are maintained in `practice/catalogue-extra.mjs`. `practice/catalogue.mjs` applies shared publishing defaults, concatenates both sets and builds `byId` from the complete collection. The distribution is 10 solo, 14 two-player, 13 three-player and 13 four-player activities. Counts in interfaces should come from this data.

The additions cover ball control, movement, service preparation, wall volleys, depth progression, direction changes, approach/volley transitions, defensive lobs, touch play, return decisions, partner calls, coverage and short custom formats. Rotations specify every player's role and the stopping condition. Shared defaults preserve the existing court/rackets/balls requirements; only the explicitly labelled wall activities require a permitted practice wall. Individual safety guidance is supplied for every addition, with shared practical guidance for the original collection.

These are original editorial practice rules, **not coach-reviewed or endorsed activities**. Existing source links are general learning references rather than evidence that an organisation designed or approved these games. Scoring restrictions and modifications are stated as practice rules. A coach should review technical suitability for a particular participant, space or access need; the content does not establish a professional review date.

Diagrams use the existing lightweight SVG schema. The 360 × 400 court has baselines at y=32/368, net at y=200, service lines at y=114/286, singles sidelines at x=80/280 and doubles sidelines at x=55/305. Yellow arrows indicate ball direction, blue dashed arrows player movement and shaded T regions targets. Arrows are schematic rather than a depiction of bounce height or curved flight; overheads, lobs and the two stationary ball-control exercises say so in their descriptions. Alternative shots and independent two-ball lanes are explicitly identified. Written setup and spacing instructions accompany the accessible diagram description.

The existing optional `video` metadata remains the extension point for a future reviewed local video with language, captions, transcript and credits. No video objects, fake controls or video-availability claims were added. Activities remain data independent of planner, page and print layouts, so future curated selections need not duplicate their content.

Focused data checks confirmed 50 unique IDs, non-empty required text, at least four instruction steps, minimum duration of at least four minutes, matching diagram/player counts, and in-bounds target/arrow coordinates. These checks support implementation correctness; they are not a coaching or physical-court trial. Page rendering, links, planner integration and representative visual review are checked by the owning implementation task.
